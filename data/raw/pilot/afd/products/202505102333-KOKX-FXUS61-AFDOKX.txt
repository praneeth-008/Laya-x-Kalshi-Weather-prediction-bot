High pressure remains in control through through the weekend.

.NEAR TERM /UNTIL 6 AM SUNDAY MORNING/...
The fcst is on track. High pressure will continue to slowly 
build into the region with a zonal flow aloft. A weak surface 
trough is expected to pass through, but without much of any 
consequence. Clear with light winds. Low temperatures ranging 
from the mid 40s well NW of the city and the Pine Barrens to the
mid-upper 50s in the city.

&&

.SHORT TERM /6 AM SUNDAY MORNING THROUGH SUNDAY NIGHT/...
High pressure dominates through the period with its center moving in 
during Sunday night. Mainly clear conditions for the entire period 
with winds around 10 mph or less. Mixing depth and temps aloft look 
to be similar to those of today, and even with less of a downslope 
component, anticipating highs to be similar with full sunshine. The 
main exception would be over CT where it should end up a little 
warmer this time. The clear conditions with light to calm winds will 
promote radiational cooling during the night. Lows dropping to 
around 40 well NW of the city and the Pine Barrens, with mid 50s in 
the city.

&&

.LONG TERM /MONDAY THROUGH SATURDAY/...
High pres builds offshore on Mon. Fair wx but some increasing 
cirrus. The GFS has it way up there at over 35k ft. The NBM was used 
for temps, with a return flow out of the S keeping the coasts 
cooler. 

A closed upr low over the sern CONUS slowly translates newd thru the 
middle of the week. This will transport Gulf moisture into the 
region, and allow numerous pieces of shrtwv energy to rotate into 
the area on sly flow aloft. This will produce chances for shwrs and 
a few tstms Tue-Wed. The residual upr trof over the region on Thu 
will then likely allow for addition shwrs and isold tstms to 
develop. With less widespread pcpn thru the day on Thu, sfc temps 
are expected to warm up and lead to more instability, so perhaps 
there is a better chance for tstms Thu aftn and eve than earlier in 
the week. The NBM was followed for temps. It is entirely possible 
that temps move in either direction over the coming days depending 
on the modeled impacts of shwrs and cloud cover over the area. This 
has already shown up in some run to run inconsistency.

The airmass warms Fri and Sat, with highs in the 80s modeled in the 
normally warmest locations by the NBM. At the same time, 
increasingly zonal flow aloft may advect additional shrtwv energy in 
from the W, so additional chances for shwrs and tstms are in the 
fcst.
